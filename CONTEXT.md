# Know Your Vote

A neutral voter guide for Florida's 2026 general election. It shows each voter the races and ballot questions on their ballot, who is running, what the candidates have said, and press stories about them. It describes and never recommends.

## Language

### The ballot

**Race**:
One choice of a person on the ballot, for one seat in one election.
_Avoid_: Contest

**Seat**:
The office position a race fills, such as U.S. Representative for District 27 or Broward County Commission District 2.
_Avoid_: Office (when you mean the specific position)

**Decided seat**:
A seat settled before November, so its race is not printed on the general-election ballot. There are exactly two kinds, never merged: **unopposed** and **elected in the primary**.
_Avoid_: Already decided (as a term), closed race

**Ballot question**:
Anything a voter answers yes or no, covering both ballot measures and judicial retention.

**Ballot measure**:
A proposed law or constitutional change put to voters. Judicial retention is not one.
_Avoid_: Measure (for retention), issue

**Amendment**:
A ballot measure that changes the Florida Constitution.

**Judicial retention**:
A yes/no ballot question on whether a sitting judge keeps their seat.

### Candidates

**Candidate**:
A person who qualified for a race we cover.

**Ballot status**:
Whether a candidate's name is printed on the ballot (**on the ballot**), they can only be written in (**write-in**), or they are not in the race (**excluded**).
_Avoid_: Tier

**Off the ballot**:
A candidate who will not appear in November for any reason: defeated in the primary, disqualified, withdrew, removed, moved to a local race, or deceased.
_Avoid_: Withdrawn (unless they actually withdrew)

**Withdrawn**:
A candidate who chose to leave the race. A subset of off the ballot, and never used for someone defeated in the primary.

**Incumbent**:
The candidate who currently holds the seat.

**Running mate**:
The candidate who runs jointly with another on one ballot line, such as a lieutenant governor.

**Candidate lead**:
A person who may belong in a race we cover but is not yet confirmed. An operator checks each one before it becomes a candidate.

### What we publish

**Brief**:
The reviewed, published content about one candidate in one race: their stated positions on the race's issues, with sources.
_Avoid_: Profile, candidate summary

**Listing**:
The names-only view of a race: who is on the ballot, with no brief.
_Avoid_: Roster (as the view), names-only brief

**Candidate page**:
The page a voter reads about one candidate. It shows a brief when the race is published and a listing when it is only listed.
_Avoid_: Profile page

**Publication state**:
How far a race or ballot question has gone toward the public, in this order: **draft**, **in review**, **listed** (names or ballot text only), **published** (brief or resource ladder shown).
_Avoid_: Tier, listed tier

**Held measure**:
A listed ballot measure whose YES and NO columns are deliberately withheld.

**Review queue**:
The operator's inbox of items waiting to be approved or rejected before they reach the public. It is not the same thing as the "in review" publication state.
_Avoid_: Review (on its own)

**Correction**:
A change to content after it has been published.

**Correction hold**:
A published race paused while a correction is made.

**Report an error**:
How a voter tells us something on the site is wrong.
_Avoid_: Correction (for the voter's message)

**Reminder correction**:
The follow-up message sent when a reminder carried a wrong date.

**Content freeze**:
The window before Election Day when published content changes only by correction.

### Positions and claims

**Issue**:
A subject a race is argued over. A **spine issue** is shared by every candidate in the race; a **candidate issue** belongs to one candidate.
_Avoid_: Topic

**Policy area**:
One of the fixed, broad subject groups (such as economy or education) that issues and stories are filed under.

**Sub-issue**:
A narrower subject within a policy area, used to tag stories.

**Position**:
A candidate's stated stance on an issue, in their own words, with a source. When they have said nothing, the position is **no stated position found**.
_Avoid_: Stance (for candidates), view

**Claim**:
One sourced statement in a brief, in one of three kinds: a **verifiable fact**, a **stated position**, or an **outside opinion**.

### Ballot measure resources

**Measure resource**:
A piece of outside material (document, article, video or audio) about a ballot measure, filed as supporting, opposing or neutral.
_Avoid_: Argument (for the resource), case for/against

**Resource ladder**:
The YES and NO columns of measure resources, each ordered by rung from most to least credible.
_Avoid_: Credibility ladder

**Rung**:
A resource's place on the ladder, fixed by what kind of thing it is, never by judging the item: **official**, **research**, **reporting**, **advocacy**, **commentary**.
_Avoid_: Tier, Positions (as the advocacy rung's name)

### Where a voter is

**Voter**:
The person using the site.
_Avoid_: User, visitor

**Coverage**:
The places the guide serves. A place is **in coverage** or not.
_Avoid_: Coverage (for how much press a candidate gets)

**Covered county**:
A Florida county the guide fully serves, with its county races, elections office and local news. Coverage grows only by adding covered counties.

**Metro**:
The urban area a covered county belongs to, used to group local news.

**District**:
A numbered electoral area whose voters elect one seat: a congressional, state legislative, county commission or school board district.

**Covered district**:
A congressional district with any part inside a covered county. Districts are never chosen directly.

**Statewide race**:
A race every Florida voter has on their ballot, such as governor.

**Resolve**:
Turning a voter's address into their district and county, which tells us which races are on their ballot. The address is used once and never kept.

**Race scope**:
Which races a page is currently showing: all, statewide only, one county, or one district.

### Dates

**Key date**:
A deadline or voting day for the election, such as the registration deadline, the vote-by-mail request deadline, the start and end of early voting, or Election Day.
_Avoid_: Election event (in prose)

**Reminder**:
A message a voter signed up for that arrives a set time before a key date.

### News

**Story**:
One piece of press or official coverage shown on the site, linked to the publisher and never re-hosted.
_Avoid_: Article, news item (in prose), post

**Outlet**:
A publisher whose stories we collect.
_Avoid_: Feed, publication

**Source**:
The attributable origin of a story or claim. Its kind is **reporting**, **opinion**, **official document** or **from the candidate**. No source, no story.
_Avoid_: News report, official record, campaign website (as kind labels)

**Lean**:
An outlet's political lean as published by an independent rating agency. It is disclosed on the outlet's page and never scored. It is in one of four states: **rated**, **unrated** (no agency covers the outlet), **N/A** (lean doesn't apply), **pending** (we haven't reviewed it yet).
_Avoid_: Bias, bias score

**Press attention**:
How many stories name each candidate in a race. Unevenness is shown with its context and never evened out.
_Avoid_: Coverage, coverage balance

**Source check**:
The test run before calling uneven press attention real: did our pipeline miss something (a **pipeline gap**, which we fix) or does the press genuinely cover one candidate more (a **real-world skew**, which we show)?

**News sweep**:
The scheduled collection of new stories from every outlet.

### Fairness

**Neutrality**:
The rule that governs our own conduct: no bias through framing, selection, padding or trimming. It never corrects the world.

**Balance Audit**:
The check that a race's briefs give every candidate comparable treatment before the race can be published.

**Symmetric Scrutiny Log**:
The record a Balance Audit produces for each race.

### People and agents

**Operator**:
Anyone with access to the admin console.
_Avoid_: Admin (as a person), reviewer

**Founder**:
The one person who makes founder-gate decisions.

**Founder gate**:
A decision only the founder may make, such as setting an outlet's lean or making a live database change.

**Research agents**:
The four agents that produce briefs: the **Profiler**, the **Recorder**, the **Fact-Checker** and the **Orchestrator**.
_Avoid_: S-pipeline, generation pipeline

**Routines**:
The scheduled jobs that keep published content current: **R1 news sweep**, **R2 logistics checks**, **R3 election notices**, **R4 ops digest**, **R5 candidate leads**, and the **watchdog**.
_Avoid_: Refresh agents, freshness layer, contact refresher

**Know Your Vote**:
The product.
_Avoid_: Civic Awareness Project, CAP (the project's earlier name, kept only in old identifiers)

## Relationships

- A **covered county** contains parts of one or more **covered districts**. A **voter** is **resolved** to exactly one district and county.
- A **race** fills one **seat**, has one **publication state**, and has one or more **candidates**. A **decided seat**'s race is not on the November ballot.
- A **published** race shows a **brief** per candidate. A **listed** race shows a **listing**.
- A **brief** holds **positions** on the race's **issues**. Each position is backed by **claims** with **sources**.
- A **ballot measure** is **published** with a **resource ladder** or **listed** with ballot text only (a **held measure** when its columns are withheld).
- A **story** comes from one **outlet**, has one **source** kind, and may name one or more **candidates**. **Lean** belongs to the outlet, never to the story.

## Flagged ambiguities

- "Coverage" was used for both the places we serve and how much press a candidate gets. Resolved: **coverage** is places only, and press counts are **press attention**.
- "Tier" was used six ways. Resolved: **ballot status**, **publication state**, **spine/candidate issue** and **rung** replace it. "Tier" survives only for source tiers inside the research agents' allowlists.
- "Withdrawn" covered candidates defeated in the primary. Resolved: **off the ballot** is the umbrella, and **withdrawn** means only someone who left the race.
- "Positions" named both a candidate's stance and a ladder rung. Resolved: the rung is **advocacy**.
- "Correction" had four meanings. Resolved: **correction**, **correction hold**, **report an error** and **reminder correction**.
- Briefs were described as "retired" (2026-09-06) and later returned. **Brief** is a live term; 36 races are published.
