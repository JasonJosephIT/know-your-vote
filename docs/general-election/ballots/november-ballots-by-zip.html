What this run found

Each of these changes something the app currently does or assumes.

  • MapFlorida redrew its congressional districts in May 2026, and the app's ZIP seed predates it. HB 1-D was signed 4 May, the state Supreme Court let it stand on 10 June, and federal qualifying was moved to 12 June so candidates filed under the new lines. The seed was built from the Census 2024-map file, so 133 of 235 covered ZIPs now resolve to a different district set. Broward's ZIPs, for example, now fall in FL-20, 22, 24, 25 and 26; the seed says 20, 23, 24 and 25. The candidate file already uses the new numbering, so a voter looking up FL-23 in Broward would be shown a Palm Beach race.
  • FL-10FL-10 will not appear on any ballot. Its only qualified candidate is marked unopposed with no write-in, and Florida keeps unopposed candidates off the general ballot entirely (F.S. 101.151(7)). The race page should say the seat was filled without an election rather than compare one candidate. The same applies to state senate districts 4 and 16 and to 28 state house districts.
  • CodesTwo status codes the parser has never seen are in the file: XTL "Transferred to Local" on seven legislative filers and DEC "Deceased" on one circuit judge. The parser fails closed on an unknown code, so the next live run stops on the first XTL row unless both are mapped to excluded first. One new party code too: ASP, American Solidarity Party.
  • SenateThe U.S. Senate race is a three-way line: Ashley Moody (Republican), Angie Nixon (Democratic), Neil Gillespie (no party affiliation). The demo fixture guessed four candidates.
  • SharedNine items are identical for every Florida voter, which is what the ballot-first landing page already renders: Governor, U.S. Senate, Attorney General, CFO, Agriculture Commissioner, three amendments, and the retention question for Justice Muñiz. Everything below that line depends on the ZIP.
  • CountyThe county half of the ballot is not derivable yet. County commission, school board, county court, and municipal questions are filed with the county Supervisor of Elections, not the state. Orange lists the 2026 general sample ballot as "Not available"; Broward mails domestic vote-by-mail ballots 24 September to 1 October, which is when the four counties normally post precinct ballots.

How this was built, and what it is not

Districts. Census 2020 ZCTA-to-block relationship (Florida rows of the 1.06 GB national file) joined to the enacted plan's block assignment file, EOGPCRP2026, from the Florida Senate. A district counts when it covers at least 5% of the ZIP's land, the same rule the existing seed uses. State senate and house districts come from the Census 2024 legislative-district relationship files; those were not redrawn.
Candidates. The Division of Elections candidate list for election 20261103-GEN, all six office groups, fetched today. Only Qualified and Unopposed rows appear; a write-in qualifier becomes a blank line; a lone unopposed candidate is not printed.
Judicial. Retention names are the DCA rows in the same file. Circuit judge runoffs are groups with exactly two qualified candidates left after the August primary.
Not here. County commission, school board, county judge, special district and municipal contests and questions. Ballot order and exact ballot titles, which the state certifies later. Precinct-level splits finer than ZIP.
Files. docs/general-election/ballots/ holds the per-ZIP manifest, the ZIP-to-district CSV, the public candidate roster, and the block assignment file. The raw DoE export carries candidate addresses and is not committed.
Sources: dos.elections.myflorida.com candidate list (20261103-GEN); flsenate.gov Congressional Redistricting page (EOGPCRP2026); www2.census.gov rel2020 relationship files; Ballotpedia and the Division of Elections amendment booklet for measure titles; county Supervisor of Elections sites for sample-ballot status. Counts are as of 7 September 2026. Candidate order shown is file order, not ballot order.
