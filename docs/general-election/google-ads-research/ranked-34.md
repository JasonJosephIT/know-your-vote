Updated October 13, 2026 after the user authorized edits based on [four-region public listening](regional-listening/README.md). The [inventory](first-layer-34.md) and [CSV](first-layer-34.csv) contain the revised executions. The original 29 slots and five selected story assets are preserved; no new asset is added.

## Ranking basis

This is an **editorial priority for individual video development**, not measured ad performance, a ranking of voter concerns, or publication order. It favors a research-supported question, a complete standalone scene, a distinct content format, a useful reveal and honest product scope. A broadly relatable premise can still have a weak execution; related explainer and skit assets remain separate rather than being counted twice as one ranked theme.

The user's comedy/content direction matters: five original-piece executions and all five selected character stories make this shortlist. Pure product demonstrations and calm explainers remain in the 34. Source demand supports the Receipts Guy learning task; it does not prove that the joke increases retention. The celebrity-politician setup is a creative choice supported by an office-awareness question, not a measured celebrity-recall finding.

The evidence IDs below refer to [sources.csv](regional-listening/sources.csv). Shared-event clusters and survey limitations remain as documented there. No upvote-based popularity score or invented conversion forecast is used.

### 1. Receipts Guy — asset 32

**Scene:** Dev pours shopping receipts onto a cafe table: “You wanted receipts.” Jo: “For that candidate quote.” She opens the original source. Dev quietly retrieves the seven-muffin receipt: “This one's personal.”

**Why first:** It combines a visible comedy setup, a clear source-checking action and a product behavior that can be demonstrated truthfully. Guide/source questions are direct in the research. One short source close-up is enough; a second parody format would add little.

**Revision applied:** The source insert must show attribution and context, not imply that a linked candidate statement independently proves a record. Keep the original comic ending. Companion originals: 02/15/19. Evidence: O02, M05.

### 2. Apartment Tour: Everything Costs Extra — asset 17

**Scene:** Ray films a tour of an apartment he is still considering. Dev describes its view of a parking lot as a premium amenity. Ray spots development outside: “Who decides what gets approved around here?” Jo points to the planning record; Dev labels the view “under development.”

**Why second:** It fills the biggest subject gap—housing and growth—through a familiar creator format and one concrete public-decision question.

**Revision applied:** Housing drives the scene instead of an abstract unpacked government map. Separate private asking prices and fees from public land-use decisions. Do not claim a local office sets private rent or guarantees cheaper housing. The shared fictional story can end at how to find the relevant authority; actual project explanations belong in regional 18/23. Evidence: T01/T04/T10, M03/M07, O03/O06, J06, E05.

### 3. Political Celebrity Trivia Night — asset 30

**Scene:** Ray dominates trivia about a fictional headline politician. Mel asks who sets his school calendar. Ray freezes, then asks to phone a friend. Dev produces a toy telephone. A brief sourced answer identifies the district school-board role.

**Why third:** It preserves the requested celebrity-politician angle and gives the ensemble a recognizable starting point. The quiz failure leads into an everyday office question without endorsing or attacking a real candidate.

**Revision applied:** Keep one office question in this video, rather than packing flooding, rates and consumer law into the quiz. Those remain in separate linked pieces. Original companion: 26; onward stories: 04/31 and 18. Evidence: E03, M05, O01. These support learning needs, not celebrity recall or predicted entertainment results.

### 4. The Source Friend Gets Corrected — asset 34

**Scene:** Jo confidently shares her old county's official page. Dev notices the heading. Jo accepts the correction. Ray, applying what he learned: “Check the county. Check the date.” Mel labels the old bookmark “retired.”

**Why fourth:** County/location confusion gives the story a practical purpose; Jo being wrong makes the recurring cast less predictable.

**Revision applied:** Show a page-heading mistake rather than stamp one fixed schedule into every region. Label the character's counties, demonstrate the check and give no universal local dates. A county-specific instructional export would instead be a version of 08/20. Evidence: O10, J04, F03.

### 5. Commute Update: Still Here — asset 06 — regional

**Scene:** Ray records a commuter update from the same spot as before: “I've become a local landmark.” Jo asks who operates this service or owns this road, then opens the appropriate source. Dev offers Ray a commemorative plaque.

**Why fifth:** Separate transportation situations appear across the regional research; a creator's running update makes the question a complete scene.

**Revision applied:** One local issue per cut. A late bus, train wait or roadwork coordination problem must use its own operator/owner and funding explanation. Do not suggest one office can guarantee a shorter commute. Evidence: T02/T03, M01, O06/E06, J02/E07.

### 6. Power Bill Unboxing — asset 28 — regional

**Scene:** Ray unveils his power bill as if it is a new gadget. Dev: “What are the new features?” Jo separates usage from rates and opens the source for the actual approval body. Dev searches the empty box for a charger.

**Why sixth:** Bills provide a concrete household situation, while the rate-versus-usage distinction gives the scene a useful reveal.

**Revision applied:** The provider determines the governance explanation; a bill increase alone does not establish its cause. Use a clearly illustrative bill or properly cleared real material. Jacksonville/JEA is the first research fit, with a Tampa Electric version next; other areas require their confirmed provider. Evidence: T09, J01/J05, F04.

### 7. Budget Unboxing: This Decision Has a Paper Trail — asset 23 — regional

**Scene:** Dev unboxes an actual local budget or agenda like a product release. Ray assumes a proposal is already approved. Jo checks the dated record and vote: “Different stages.” Dev asks about the warranty.

**Why seventh:** Public-spending questions now have a tangible object, one misunderstanding and an evidence-based correction.

**Revision applied:** Replace the vague neighborhood-agenda treatment with one verified spending decision. State whether it is proposed, voted, appropriated or implemented. Identify the funding body; do not imply that stadium spending caused school closures, or turn anonymous corruption claims into a news report. Tampa and Jacksonville are the first research fits. Evidence: T05, M01/M04, O05, J02/J07/J08, E07.

### 8. GRWM for School Pickup — asset 31

**Scene:** Ray assembles his school-pickup outfit and asks who sets the calendar. Mel supplies the school-board explanation. Ray tries to wear the wall calendar: “This was the only date that matched my shirt.”

**Why eighth:** A GRWM reel is a distinct form with a visual payoff and the established cast.

**Revision applied:** Keep one calendar question, with no invented closure or district date. The research's funding/staffing concerns can inform the separate 04/23 explanations; they do not specifically validate the calendar joke. Evidence: O05/O09. Original companion: 04.

### 9. The Fee That Appears at Checkout — asset 29

**Scene:** One fictional purchase gains an unexpected fee. Dev reads the receipt like a review: “What does this feature do?” Jo looks at the written breakdown and appropriate official consumer guidance. Dev reviews the fee: “No instructions included.”

**Why ninth:** Surprise pricing and sales-pressure situations give the restored consumer subject a clear individual scene.

**Revision applied:** One ordinary complaint category and one agency route per execution. No named-business allegation, fraud finding or emergency-gouging claim. A later storm/gouging version must establish the separate legal conditions. This is not a complaint-submission feature in KYV. Evidence: M09, J09, O11, F05.

### 10. Follow the Puddle — asset 33 — regional

**Scene:** Ray “knows a guy” who can fix a puddle. Dev arrives with a tiny float. Jo follows the location's verified drainage responsibilities. Dev points to a toy bowl: “Mine has one department.”

**Why tenth:** The selected miniature-prop joke supports a physical office question, but the local factual requirements and uneven fresh evidence make it more conditional than the leading scenes.

**Revision applied:** Use a checked drainage location and make responsibility legible without a long lecture. Tampa first, Central Florida next; Miami's current source here is supplementary, and Jacksonville needs fresher local flood-specific feedback. The joke targets the shortcut and tiny prop, not flood damage. Evidence: T01, O07, M08. Original companion: 27.

## What moves forward

The top 10 are concrete briefs for storyboarding. Keep the same characters, wardrobe, props and source-checking behavior, while allowing each piece to stay in its own content form. Add graphics or screen recordings when they explain a role or make a source readable; genre-switching is optional.

A light continuity path is **30 → 17 → 06 → 23 → 32 → 34**: famous names, everyday housing, commuting, a public record, source access, then correcting an assumption. The original five-story path **30 → 31 → 32 → 33 → 34** remains available. These are editorial/public-playlist relationships, not a requirement that viewers watch earlier episodes or a paid sequencing implementation.

Creative rank does not schedule publication. Deadline pieces may need earlier treatment if later authorized for delivery, and every factual insert must be current when exported. No filming, generated videos, campaign activation, spend or delivery changes are performed by this update.
