## The user's format direction

Mixed media can mean footage plus animation/screen recordings/graphics, or multiple content forms inside a piece. Either can work. Neither is mandatory. A single reported explainer, direct product demonstration or skit can remain in one form when that best serves the idea. Continuity comes from the cast, visual identity, narrative viewpoint and repeated questions; it does not require the same editing format every time.

Change format when it makes evidence legible, makes an institution's role visible, or delivers the joke. Do not insert a podcast, fake news clip or animated transition merely to increase the number of styles. Retention benefits are hypotheses to test with produced edits, not established facts. A content-first piece still has clear KYV attribution; style does not hide who made it.

There are two parallel creative families, both intended for the first layer:

- **Original pieces 01–29:** useful questions, evidence, demonstrations and explainers. Some are direct ads; some work naturally as social or mixed-media content.
- **Story additions 30–34:** recurring-character videos with their own setups and endings. Four are deliberate alternative executions of original subjects; the fifth connects source-checking and county information.

These are 34 core pieces, not 34 separately budgeted ad campaigns. Each region-specific export is a version of its parent piece; it does not create another idea in this inventory. Campaign grouping, final edits and delivery settings can be decided later. This document concerns creative selection and the relationships between pieces.

## How the evaluation works

**Keep** means the creative premise works and can move to a detailed storyboard. **Rework** means preserve its slot, but use the revised execution below rather than the earlier wording. **Hold** means retain it in the 34, with an explicit content/timing dependency before publication. None means an asset is already produced, its destination has been inspected in production, or Google has approved it.

Editorial assessment: **26 Keep, 6 Rework, 2 Hold**. This is creative judgment based on clarity, distinctiveness, factual fit and product scope; it is not a performance forecast. Each row gives a primary treatment and an alternative where useful. An alternative is a production choice under that ID, not another asset added to the 34. Only the selected five companion videos receive additional IDs.

## Which pieces don't work as previously written?

Six require an actual change, not a cosmetic format swap:

1. **15:** The promise is ahead of established product content. Use sourced statements and explain attribution versus proof.
2. **20:** A bookmark is a small action without a motivating problem. Give the person a concrete reason to need the official link.
3. **21:** “Our guide has limits” leads with a drawback. Show the useful task and its complete-ballot referral together.
4. **23:** A generic meeting agenda doesn't establish relevance. Follow one decision and its authentic paper trail.
5. **24:** General independence language lacks a visual event. Make reading the source the event; keep the person's choice private.
6. **29:** Scams and emergency price gouging are distinct. Keep a single office-focused story, with two labeled examples rather than two competing dramatic plots.

Two remain in the production pool with a timing/content hold: **22** after Election Day and **25** after real translated content/native review. The other office pieces also require their promised factual inserts and usable explanatory destinations before delivery; “Keep” does not erase those dependencies. The package should not imply that all 34 are ready to run today.

## Story continuity and links between topics

The five story videos have a light chronological arc:

**30 → 31 → 32 → 33 → 34**

The ensemble starts with famous names, encounters an everyday office question, learns why sources matter, discovers that responsibilities can be shared, and finally applies the checking habit while Jo accepts correction. Each episode repeats enough context to stand alone.

Useful thematic routes among the originals:

- **Offices:** 26 → 04/06 → 14/18 → 27/28/29. The connecting question is “Who handles this?” No claim all those offices are on the 2026 ballot.
- **Evidence:** 02 → 10 → revised 15 → 19 → revised 24. The connecting behavior is finding, reading and comparing the source.
- **Voting preparation:** 01 → 12/21 → 08 → 05/16/11 → revised 20. Each piece answers one practical question.
- **Evergreen local learning:** 17 → 18 → revised 23 → 22 when the timing fits. These are education links, not a comprehensive representative directory.

Callbacks can carry between families: a wall calendar connects 04/31/05; a quote and source connects 02/32/15; a puddle location connects 27/33/23. A prop should reward recognition, never conceal the actual subject. Reuse Ray's cardboard badge only where the invented-title joke helps.

These arrows describe narrative and public-content relationships, not a promise that paid viewers will see ads in that order. For Google-classified election ads, keep delivery within the allowed geography/context criteria; do not implement story sequencing with video-view remarketing lists. [Google political-content policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6014595?hl=en). Shared public playlists and optional pinned links provide continuity without making prior exposure a prerequisite.
