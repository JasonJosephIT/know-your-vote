# Who handles that? — sourced Florida office table

Checked 2026-10-13. Applies to **Miami-Dade, Broward, Hillsborough and Orange**, the complete county coverage list in `src/lib/counties.ts`. Responsibility is not the same as personal control over a service, and an elected office is not necessarily on the November 2026 ballot. Tables below distinguish those questions.

For any pothole/traffic-light example, establish who owns the specific road first. Location within a county does not prove county ownership. State highways → FDOT; municipal roads → city; county roads → county public works. Private roads and some expressways require different contacts. Use a general role explainer until ownership is known.

## Which relevant seats reach the 2026 general ballot?

The [state office list](https://dos.fl.gov/elections/candidates-committees/offices-up-for-election) confirms Governor/Lt. Governor, Agriculture Commissioner, Attorney General and CFO are up in 2026. Appointed FDOT/PSC/water-board positions are not direct ballot contests. Ordinary sheriff/appraiser/tax-collector/supervisor cycles do not justify a 2026 “on your ballot” message; special vacancies can differ. Orange clerk is one such county-specific exception.

The table is limited to relevant covered county races, not all offices filed in each county. Candidate financial-reporting `Active`/`Inactive` prefixes do not establish whether a contest is printed: inspect the qualifying status (`Runoff`, `Qualified`, `Elected`, `Unopposed`) and current sample ballot. ## Corrected copy sketches and destination requirements

- “Who helps fund local buses?” → county-specific transit explainer → relevant public county race, when there is one.
- “Who checks gas-pump accuracy?” → FDACS/Agriculture explainer → public Agriculture race.
- “Who sets your school calendar?” → district school-board explainer → applicable public contest or official current board information.
- “Who values property? Who sets rates?” → appraiser/rate-setter/collector explainer → applicable commission/school-board contests plus official contacts.
- “Some local decisions are made by appointed boards.” → explain the appointment chain, not a nonexistent board election.

All sketches remain held until the explanatory content and onward links exist. For settled races use “learn about the office,” never “compare your November choices.” Google review may classify a destination featuring state/federal candidates or measures as election-related even when a local-office headline is generic.
