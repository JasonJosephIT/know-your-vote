Working creative draft, 2026-10-13. These are individual skits with a setup, turn and ending, not campaign themes. They translate the previous 25 ideas plus the four restored ideas into specific videos; number 30 adds an ensemble reversal. Numbers identify sketches, not predicted performance. No videos have been filmed or generated, and no campaigns have been launched.

## The recurring cast and story

Working title: **The Florida Group Chat**. Four fictional friends encounter everyday questions at a cafe, a kitchen table, outside school, in a neighborhood and on local transit. Locations can vary by region without assigning political attitudes to a place.

- **Ray — confidently familiar.** Remembers headline politicians like celebrities. Assumes he understands local government. Oversized sunglasses, cream bowling shirt, green collar. Refrain: “I know a guy.” He is curious and useful, not the permanent fool.
- **Jo — the source friend.** Quiet, dry delivery. Pulls up the original document rather than settling arguments by authority. Green overshirt and notebook. Refrain: “Where did that come from?” Jo can make mistakes too.
- **Mel — the organizer.** Makes calendars and checklists for everything. Olive cap, gold clipboard. Refrain: “Put a date on it.” Their organizational ambition is the joke, not the importance of deadlines.
- **Dev — the literalist.** Takes expressions physically: brings receipts, translates a flag, builds a miniature government map. Burnt-orange work jacket and tote. Refrain: “You said…” Dev eventually catches what everyone else misses.

The running story: Ray starts by knowing the famous names, learns to ask which office, then starts checking sources. Mel and Dev grow beyond their initial habits; Jo learns to accept corrections. Each video works without watching the others. A prop or a line can return, but no viewer needs a recap.

Use exaggerated fictional mannerisms and silhouettes. For animation, follow `docs/design.md`'s Green/Cream-led funk palette and illustration skin tokens. Keep candidate images separate from character illustrations. For live action, maintain the same costume, props and personality. Humour targets guessing, overcomplication and misunderstandings rather than a party, official, community or voter demographic.

## Content treatment

Default to vertical 20–35 second videos with a cold open, one comic escalation, one clear reveal and a final joke. This is a draft duration range, not a timed edit. Let the parody format govern camera work: a reaction reel, unboxing, cooking tutorial, mockumentary, podcast excerpt, screen recording or cinematic trailer. Some videos include another format inside them, such as friends reacting to Ray's own influencer video.

KYV can appear as the tool a friend uses, an account attribution or a source note. Office education uses sourced explanatory inserts, not fabricated screenshots of a module that already exists. These source inserts would be produced with the video and posted in its caption. Do not put the same sales speech at the end of all 30. The brand can become familiar through characters, color, attribution and repeated useful behavior.

State Farm describes Jake's social output as character-led lifestyle storytelling, a useful reference for recognizable character continuity: [official description](https://newsroom.statefarm.com/jake-from-state-farm-tiktok/). These scripts use original characters, dialogue and situations. The exact Instagram example and “Dyckel” reference were not supplied; their identity remains an optional creative refinement, not an assumed brief.

The current verbal guide says 2026 profiles emphasize sourced candidate statements; it does not establish published records or fact-checks in every brief (`docs/voice-and-tone.md`). Product shots therefore show only live verified content. Keep complete-ballot links to the official county sample ballot. Jacksonville/Duval has no selected local-race coverage in the current app; its local education sketches need their own source inserts and official referrals. Broad Tampa Bay/Central Florida geography does not expand Hillsborough/Orange coverage.

## The thirty sketches

### 01 — The Ballot Prep Confessional

**Format:** Selfie storytime, interrupted by a friend. **Cast:** Ray, Jo. **Setting:** Kitchen or neighborhood cafe; all regions.

**Open:** Ray, to camera: “I came completely prepared.” Jo holds up an untouched official sample ballot: “You brought a pen.”

**Turn:** Ray reveals more pens, then a tiny pencil. Jo slides over sourced information for the covered contests and the county's complete sample ballot.

**Ending:** Ray: “So the fourth pen was excessive?” Jo: “The first three were also pens.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Preparation means reading the contests, not collecting stationery. Brief real product insert; no whole-ballot promise.

### 02 — Receipts Guy

**Format:** Food-review reel with a cutaway. **Cast:** Dev, Jo. **Setting:** Cafe; all regions.

**Open:** Dev: “You wanted receipts.” He pours grocery, parking and lunch receipts over the table.

**Turn:** Jo: “For that candidate quote.” Dev hunts for an impossibly long receipt. Jo opens the original quoted source on her phone.

**Ending:** Dev quietly takes back a receipt: “This one's personal. I bought seven muffins.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** A source is the evidence behind a particular statement. Real quoted-source insert; no claim that a receipt establishes truth.

### 03 — Cooking With Ballot Language

**Format:** Cooking tutorial parody. **Cast:** Mel, Ray. **Setting:** Kitchen; statewide content in all regions.

**Open:** Mel: “Today we're breaking down an amendment.” Ray sets out flour and a whisk.

**Turn:** Mel arranges three cards like ingredients: current rule, proposed change, arguments and sources. Ray tries to put the current-rule card in a mixing bowl.

**Ending:** Mel: “We're explaining it.” Ray: “So preheat to…?” Mel switches off his oven timer.

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Explain one real published 2026 amendment. This is a neutral breakdown, never a recipe for how to vote.

### 04 — GRWM for School Pickup

**Format:** Get-ready-with-me reel with an off-camera interruption. **Cast:** Ray, Mel. **Setting:** School pickup preparation; Hillsborough or Orange first.

**Open:** Ray accessorizes: keys, sunglasses, snack bag. “School's closed tomorrow. I assume the governor changed my calendar.”

**Turn:** Mel: “Have you checked your district school board?” A short sourced calendar/board insert interrupts the outfit montage.

**Ending:** Ray adds a giant wall calendar to his outfit. Mel: “You can read it without wearing it.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Local school boards have calendar responsibilities subject to state law. Education first; link only an applicable public race if there is one.

### 05 — Calendar Unboxing

**Format:** ASMR unboxing parody. **Cast:** Mel, Dev. **Setting:** Kitchen; covered counties first.

**Open:** Mel whispers: “Today we're unboxing my election reminders.” A box sits inside a box inside another box.

**Turn:** The last box contains a paper saying “Calendar file.” Dev shows the actual verified download on his phone.

**Ending:** Mel, still whispering: “This could have been a download.” Dev: “It is a download.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Show the real verified calendar option. Other counties use appropriate official dates; do not assume a file contains dates it lacks.

### 06 — The Transit Reaction Video

**Format:** Ray's creator reel shown within Jo's reaction video. **Cast:** Ray, Jo. **Setting:** Miami transit platform first; separate HART/LYNX/JTA versions.

**Open:** In the inner video, Ray wears a cardboard badge: “Reporting live as your Transportation Person.”

**Turn:** Jo freezes the frame: “Which system? Which board? What does that badge mean?” A clean diagram identifies the actual operator and decision-making roles.

**Ending:** Unfreeze. Ray turns over his badge: “It came with the clipboard.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** The invented title is the joke. The diagram is regional and factual; no claim that a single elected person controls service frequency.

### 07 — The Ballot Nature Documentary

**Format:** Mock nature documentary. **Cast:** Dev narrator, Ray subject, Jo interruption. **Setting:** Kitchen; selected county contests in covered areas.

**Open:** Dev whispers: “Here we observe the voter scrolling beyond the statewide races.” Ray opens a selected local contest.

**Turn:** Dev crouches behind a houseplant with binoculars. Jo: “He's six feet away.”

**Ending:** Ray looks at the camera: “Can I read in peace?” Dev: “The subject has detected us.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Give selected local races attention without inventing undervote statistics or shaming people who leave a contest blank.

### 08 — The Wrong County Travel Vlog

**Format:** Travel-vlog parody. **Cast:** Ray, Jo. **Setting:** All four regions; Duval uses official guidance.

**Open:** Ray announces his early-voting plan using a saved screenshot. Jo points to its county heading: “That's not your county.”

**Turn:** Ray: “But the palm tree looked familiar.” Jo opens his actual Supervisor's current page.

**Ending:** He pans to a second palm: “This one?” Jo lowers his camera.

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Check jurisdiction and current dates. No common statewide optional schedule; verified official county link in the caption.

### 09 — Tax Bill Unboxing: Three Different Jobs

**Format:** Unboxing with miniature staging. **Cast:** Dev, Jo. **Setting:** Tampa or Central Florida first; roles relevant statewide.

**Open:** Dev opens his tax bill: “Which person did all this?”

**Turn:** Jo sets out labeled figures for property valuation, tax-rate decisions and collection. Dev gives them one enormous shared hat. Jo separates the roles.

**Ending:** Dev: “Fine. Three hats.” Jo: “The number of taxing authorities can vary.” Dev slowly opens a hat cupboard.

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Distinguish responsibilities, never claim a universal three-person or four-person tax bill.

### 10 — The Group Chat Forwarding Olympics

**Format:** Chat screen recording intercut with faces. **Cast:** Ray, Jo, Mel, Dev. **Setting:** All regions.

**Open:** Ray sends a bold election-related claim. A typing bubble erupts, then three forwarding arrows.

**Turn:** Jo sends: “Original source?” Everything stops. Ray starts typing “Someone sent…” then deletes it.

**Ending:** His next message: “Original source: me receiving it.” Dev reacts with a magnifying glass emoji.

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Pause before passing a claim along. Use a fictional generic claim rather than spreading an actual accusation as the hook.

### 11 — The Election Prep Heist

**Format:** Cinematic caper trailer. **Cast:** Ensemble. **Setting:** Kitchen; all regions.

**Open:** Dramatic music. Mel rolls out a blueprint: “We need a time, a location and the right information.”

**Turn:** Ray dons a disguise. Jo: “It's voting preparation, Ray.” They check the official location and voting details.

**Ending:** Dev opens a briefcase containing the seven muffins from video 02. Mel: “Finally, someone understood the assignment.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Ordinary lawful preparation receives exaggerated movie treatment. No secret-entry imagery at a polling place or promise of skipping queues.

### 12 — The Fence Interview

**Format:** Two-person street interview with split screen. **Cast:** Ray, Dev, Jo. **Setting:** Covered county neighborhood.

**Open:** Ray interviews Dev across a fence: “Same county, identical ballot. Correct?”

**Turn:** Jo uses the official sample ballots and labeled districts to show where relevant contests differ.

**Ending:** Ray: “But we share a fence.” Dev: “We also share a mower. That hasn't helped.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Geography affects contests. Never imply a fence necessarily marks a district boundary or KYV maps every local contest.

### 13 — First-Time Voter Orientation

**Format:** First-day orientation parody. **Cast:** Dev newcomer, Mel, Ray. **Setting:** Central Florida courtyard first; adaptable statewide.

**Open:** Mel has an elaborate welcome checklist. Dev: “I have a question.” Ray: “Save it for the advanced class.”

**Turn:** Dev asks an ordinary voting-process question. Mel uses official guidance. Ray raises his hand: “Actually, same question.”

**Ending:** Dev hands Ray a second welcome folder.

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Asking is normal. The newcomer is not the joke. Use accurate eligibility/deadline guidance; no November registration promise after closure.

### 14 — Gas Pump ASMR

**Format:** Close-up satisfaction/ASMR video interrupted by dialogue. **Cast:** Dev, Jo, Ray. **Setting:** Station forecourt; all regions.

**Open:** Dev films the pump display ticking: “Who checks whether this measures accurately?” Ray: “The Transportation Person.”

**Turn:** Jo points to sourced FDACS inspection information. Dev peels Ray's recycled cardboard badge from the camera frame.

**Ending:** Ray: “My badge has range.” Jo: “It has cardboard.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Agriculture/Consumer Services inspection responsibilities; no inspection demonstration or claim the office sets pump prices.

### 15 — The One-Question Podcast

**Format:** Overproduced podcast excerpt. **Cast:** Ray host, Jo guest, Dev producer. **Setting:** Kitchen turned studio; all regions.

**Open:** Ray: “Candidate says X. Therefore X has happened.” Jo: “Those are different claims.”

**Turn:** She shows what the original statement actually supports. Ray stares at his stack of question cards, all based on his assumption.

**Ending:** Dev announces: “Great episode. Forty-seven seconds. No sequel.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Attributing a statement differs from independently proving it. KYV product shots show sourced statements, not unverified record/fact-check completeness.

### 16 — Dating the Wrong Deadline

**Format:** Dating-show parody. **Cast:** Ray host; Mel and Dev as two labeled deadline characters.

**Open:** Ray: “Meet your mail-ballot deadline.” Mel's card says “Request”; Dev's says “Return.” Ray gestures at them interchangeably.

**Turn:** Both object: “Different jobs. Different dates.” Clear current official labels replace his homemade cue cards.

**Ending:** Ray: “So this is not a match.” Mel: “This is a calendar.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Requesting and receipt of a voted ballot are distinct; any displayed dates must be verified and expire appropriately.

### 17 — Moving Vlog: Unpacking the Government

**Format:** Moving/DIY reel. **Cast:** Dev, Jo, Ray. **Setting:** Tampa or Central Florida neighborhood first.

**Open:** Dev labels boxes kitchen, bedroom and “government.” Ray: “That last one's a lot of furniture.”

**Turn:** Dev unfolds a miniature map of offices. Jo identifies the actual roles using official sources.

**Ending:** Ray finds his cardboard badge in the box: “I knew I lived here.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Office education for anyone unfamiliar with local roles. Information about updating an existing registration is distinct from new registration.

### 18 — Customer Service: Which Government?

**Format:** Fictional phone-support sketch. **Cast:** Ray, Jo, Dev. **Setting:** Jacksonville version first.

**Open:** Ray performs as his own imaginary switchboard: “City department? County function? Independent authority?”

**Turn:** Dev: “Before you transfer me, what am I asking about?” Jo pulls up the relevant official responsibility page; Ray had skipped the question entirely.

**Ending:** Ray puts himself on hold. His hold music is his own humming.

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Jacksonville's consolidated government and independent authorities need a local explanation. This is Ray's invented help desk, not an impersonation of a real agency.

### 19 — Couples Therapy for Browser Tabs

**Format:** Therapy-session parody. **Cast:** Mel facilitator, Ray and Dev as personified browser tabs, Jo.

**Open:** Mel: “We're here because you refuse to share a screen.” Two tabs talk over each other from opposite chairs.

**Turn:** Jo demonstrates a real balanced comparison view for a covered race.

**Ending:** Both tabs: “Can we keep our own sources?” Jo: “That's the point.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Compare without flattening evidence or implying a preferred candidate. Characters are browser tabs, not caricatures of actual candidates.

### 20 — The Bookmark Room Tour

**Format:** Tiny-room tour/social haul. **Cast:** Dev, Jo. **Setting:** All regions; strong official-referral use for Duval.

**Open:** Dev proudly reveals a miniature shelf for “the page I always lose.”

**Turn:** Jo bookmarks the correct official Supervisor page on her phone. Dev measures the screen: “There's room for a shelf.”

**Ending:** He gives the miniature shelf a tiny miniature shelf.

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Save the right official voting-information link. Do not pretend KYV supplies an official precinct lookup for every county.

### 21 — What's Actually in the Guide? A Haul

**Format:** Honest product-haul parody. **Cast:** Ray, Jo, Mel.

**Open:** Ray opens a box labeled “Florida Guide.” “Does it contain my entire ballot?”

**Turn:** Jo lays out cards for actual covered content, then the official sample-ballot link for the complete list. Mel makes two clearly labeled piles: covered and outside scope.

**Ending:** Ray searches the box: “But does it have bubble wrap?” Jo hands him bubble wrap. He is delighted.

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Show coverage honestly. Do not turn limitations into a promise that unsupported contests are coming soon.

### 22 — The Post-Credits Scene

**Format:** Movie ending followed by a bonus scene. **Cast:** Ensemble. **Setting:** All regions after Election Day.

**Open:** Ray: “Election's over. Roll credits.” Everyone packs up.

**Turn:** Mel's next-month school and public-meeting calendars remain. Jo: “Government's still operating.”

**Ending:** Ray quietly wheels his chair back into frame: “I renewed for another season.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Continue sourced office education. Winners are not automatically current officeholders; use no unsupported live representative directory.

### 23 — Neighborhood Tour, Meeting Agenda Edition

**Format:** Property-tour parody. **Cast:** Ray host, Jo, Dev. **Setting:** Tampa or Jacksonville version first.

**Open:** Ray tours a park bench: “Excellent seating. Very open floor plan.”

**Turn:** Jo connects a real local public decision to its actual meeting agenda. Dev looks for a walk-in closet under the bench.

**Ending:** Ray: “The neighborhood has paperwork?” Jo: “Quite a lot.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Show one verified decision and body; never assume every park is county-owned or every project is on a current agenda.

### 24 — The Quiz That Won't Pick for You

**Format:** Personality-quiz creator reel. **Cast:** Ray, Jo, Mel.

**Open:** Ray: “Answer three lifestyle questions and this will choose your candidate.” Mel asks: “What if I like both coffee and tea?”

**Turn:** Jo replaces the invented quiz with actual source-backed comparisons. “Read the positions. Make your own choice.”

**Ending:** Ray holds a mug: “But can it choose my beverage?” Mel: “That is still your problem.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** KYV does not recommend a candidate based on a quiz. Ray's invented quiz is clearly a skit, not a working feature.

### 25 — The Translation Editing Fail

**Format:** Behind-the-scenes editing reel. **Cast:** Ray, Dev, Jo.

**Open:** Ray: “Spanish version complete.” He has changed only a decorative label. Dev: “The explanation is still English.”

**Turn:** Jo brings in a fully translated, natively reviewed explanation; the actual captions and sources change together.

**Ending:** Ray: “So I translated a rectangle.” Dev: “Barely.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** The joke targets superficial editing, not a language or accent. **Held for real translation work.** Spanish and Haitian Creole versions need separate native review and readable destinations; the current app is English.

### 26 — Political Celebrity Trivia Night

**Format:** Pub-quiz skit or polished comedy spot. **Cast:** Ray contestant, Mel host, Jo, Dev.

**Open:** Ray answers absurd fictional headline-politician trivia: “Dog's name? Shoe size? Favorite breakfast? Easy.”

**Turn:** Mel: “Who sets your school calendar?” Ray is silent. Jo opens a sourced office explanation.

**Ending:** Ray: “Can I call the dog?” Dev offers a toy telephone.

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Restore the celebrity-politician angle through a fictional trivia setup; no real officeholder images, endorsement or partisan comparison. Localized final question can be schools, transit or a local-government role.

### 27 — Follow the Puddle

**Format:** Neighborhood vlog interrupted by miniature-documentary graphics. **Cast:** Ray, Dev, Jo.

**Open:** Ray points to standing water after rain: “I know a guy.” Dev arrives with a tiny pool float: “I know a boat.”

**Turn:** Jo follows the local drainage path through a sourced graphic, showing that neighborhood, local and regional responsibilities can differ.

**Ending:** Dev's tiny float bobs in a bowl of water: “Mine has one department.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Identify the actual system and owner. Miami version can explain the South Florida three-tier system; other locations need their own map. No scenes of entering floodwater, no invented guarantee of flood prevention.

### 28 — The Power Bill Courtroom

**Format:** Kitchen-table courtroom parody. **Cast:** Mel judge, Ray bill holder, Dev puppeteer, Jo.

**Open:** Ray: “Who is responsible for this bill?” Dev produces two puppets: Usage and Rates.

**Turn:** Both puppets try to point at each other. Jo explains the relevant rate-setting body, separately from usage and other bill components.

**Ending:** Mel: “The fan may keep spinning. The guessing may not.” Ray lowers his cardboard gavel.

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Provider-specific rate education: PSC-jurisdiction utility, OUC or JEA. Do not imply the Governor/CFO sets every household bill or promise lower rates.

### 29 — Influencer Unboxing: The Suspicious Box

**Format:** Fake influencer unboxing inside a reaction reel. **Cast:** Ray creator, Jo reactor, Mel, Dev.

**Open:** Ray: “They say I won a free mystery box. I only have to pay to claim it.” Mel: “Then why are you calling it free?”

**Turn:** Jo pauses the clip, links official consumer-protection guidance, then briefly distinguishes scam reporting from emergency price-gouging complaints. No real solicitation or active scam link.

**Ending:** Dev opens Ray's prop box. Inside: another invoice. Ray: “Bonus content.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Attorney General consumer-protection responsibilities; price gouging has specific emergency conditions. One linked explainer can carry the detailed distinction beyond the skit.

### 30 — The Source Friend Gets Corrected

**Format:** Reaction reel with a role reversal. **Cast:** Jo, Dev, Ray, Mel.

**Open:** Jo confidently shares a voting schedule she bookmarked. Dev spots the wrong county in its heading.

**Turn:** Jo checks the current correct official page: “You're right. That's my old county.” Ray, now applying what he learned: “Check the county. Check the date.”

**Ending:** Mel sticks a note on Jo's notebook: “Source friend: accepts updates.” Jo: “I'm keeping that.”

**Useful beat / KYV role:** Checking requires correct place, date and context. This is a friend's mistaken bookmark, not a fabricated KYV error. Everyone can learn and correct themselves.

## Facts behind the explanatory inserts

Reuse the [issue-office table](issue-office.md) and [research memo](README.md) for existing county/office evidence. New supporting checks:

- South Florida drainage is shared across regional agencies, local government, drainage operators and community/property responsibilities. The pattern is not automatically the same map everywhere: [SFWMD](https://www.sfwmd.gov/our-work/flood-control).
- PSC rate jurisdiction differs for municipal/cooperative utilities: [PSC overview](https://www.psc.state.fl.us/about). OUC's commission sets its rates/policies: [OUC](https://www.ouc.com/about/who-we-are/). JEA describes its rate-setting role and PSC rate-structure review: [JEA electric disclosure](https://www.jea.com/About/Investor_Relations/Financial_Reports/ADR_--_Annual_Disclosure_Report_--_Electric_System/).
- Consumer-protection civil enforcement and specified emergency price-gouging complaints are related but distinct: [Attorney General division](https://www.myfloridalegal.com/consumer-protection), [price-gouging FAQ](https://www.myfloridalegal.com/price-gouging-hotline/price-gouging-frequently-asked-questions). Indexed official text was readable; direct access returned 403 during research.
- Jacksonville's council is the legislative body of consolidated government: [official explanation](https://www.jacksonville.gov/City-Council). Use the [current Duval early-voting page](https://www.duvalelections.gov/362/2026-General-Early-Voting-Information) for any date-bearing insert, not a superseded planning calendar.

Every shoot-ready fact card needs the exact place, provider, office, election cycle and source checked when producing it. Office sketches can teach a role even when that office is appointed or not on that year's ballot. No skit should say otherwise just to get a punchline.
